Synopsis
--------

**Objectives:** EUCAST susceptibility testing clinical breakpoints are available for temocillin with both disc diffusion and Etest methodologies. We assessed whether MIC testing added value after initial disc diffusion testing in blood culture samples tested at Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Trust.

**Methods:** We retrospectively analysed routine microbiology data for blood culture isolates of *E. coli*, *Klebsiella* spp. (excluding *K. aerogenes*) and *P. mirabilis* tested for temocillin between 1 January 2023 and 21 October 2026. Of 3009 such blood isolates tested by disc diffusion, 585 (19.4%) also had an Etest and were included. Zone diameters and MICs were reinterpreted against EUCAST Breakpoint Table v16.1 (R: zone <17 mm/MIC >16 mg/L; I otherwise). Agreement was calculated overall and by organism; logistic regression quantified P(Etest-R) by zone diameter.

**Results:** Overall categorical agreement was 84.4% (494/585). Of 457 disc-I isolates, only 7 (1.5%) were reclassified as resistant by Etest. Of 128 disc-R isolates, 84 (65.6%) were reclassified as I. The pattern held in *E. coli* (62/88, 70.5% of disc-R reclassified) and *K. pneumoniae* (21/38, 55.3%). Each 1 mm increase in zone diameter reduced the odds of an Etest-R result by 39% (OR 0.61, 95% CI 0.54–0.67, *P*<0.001).

**Conclusions:** In blood culture isolates, disc-diffusion-susceptible (I) temocillin results are highly reliable and do not need routine Etest confirmation. However, most disc-resistant (R) isolates were not confirmed resistant by Etest. Reflex MIC testing should be considered before excluding temocillin as a treatment option on a disc-resistant blood culture result alone.

Introduction
------------

Temocillin's stability against most ESBLs and AmpC enzymes makes it an attractive carbapenem-sparing option for urinary-tract infections (UTIs) caused by ESBL-/AmpC-producing Enterobacterales,^1^ with clinical cure rates similar to carbapenems in a matched case-control study (94% versus 99%).^2^ Under EUCAST Clinical Breakpoint Table v16.1, temocillin breakpoints against *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella* spp. (excluding *K. aerogenes*) and *Proteus mirabilis* are validated only for urinary-tract-source infections — reflecting temocillin's high urinary concentration^3,4^ — and the susceptible (S) category (MIC ≤0.001 mg/L/zone ≥50 mm) falls below what either disc diffusion or Etest can practically resolve. Routine results are therefore effectively a binary susceptible-with-increased-exposure (I; zone 17–49 mm) / resistant (R; zone <17 mm) call.

Prior studies report generally good, but imperfect, disc-diffusion/MIC agreement for temocillin: low major-error rates against the older, higher BSAC breakpoint;^5^ good interlaboratory category agreement using current EUCAST breakpoints, weaker for *Proteus* spp.;^7^ and, specifically relevant here, evidence that lowering the disc breakpoint to the current 17 mm EUCAST cutoff substantially reduced reported resistance and that zone-diameter reading immediately around that cutoff is prone to remeasurement variability.^6^ Whether reflex Etest confirmation is better targeted at disc-resistant isolates, disc-susceptible isolates, or neither, has not, to our knowledge, been directly reported. Blood cultures account for the great majority of paired disc diffusion/Etest temocillin testing at our institution, consistent with Etest being reserved for the clinically time-pressured setting of bacteraemia; we therefore addressed this question specifically in blood culture isolates, using paired results from a single UK laboratory (Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Trust).

Methods
-------

Microbiology results were extracted from the CHEQS laboratory information system for all temocillin tests performed between 1 January 2023 and 21 October 2026 (19 902 records, 18 985 specimens, 12 498 patients, all specimen types). Analysis was restricted to blood culture isolates of the three organism groups for which EUCAST validates a temocillin breakpoint: *E. coli* (excluding serotype O157 and mixed growth), *Klebsiella* spp. (excluding *K. aerogenes*) and *P. mirabilis*.^3^ Blood was chosen because it accounted for the great majority of paired disc diffusion/Etest testing in this cohort; other specimen types (urine, swab, body fluid, etc.) each had ≤12 paired isolates, too few for separate meaningful analysis. Of 3009 blood culture isolates of these organisms tested by disc diffusion, 585 (19.4%) also had a temocillin Etest and formed the paired cohort analysed here — a substantially higher pairing rate than the 4.6% seen across all specimen types, consistent with Etest being reserved preferentially for bacteraemic isolates. Bacteraemia with these organisms is frequently secondary to a urinary source, but this was not confirmed case-by-case and is treated as an assumption, not a settled fact, when interpreting blood isolates against a breakpoint validated for urinary-tract-source infection.

Rather than using the susceptibility category recorded at testing (which had occasional gaps), zone diameters and Etest MICs were reinterpreted directly against EUCAST v16.1 (R: MIC >16 mg/L or zone <17 mm; I: all other values).^3^ Etest "<"/">" qualifiers were stripped and MICs capped at 256 mg/L. Categorical agreement was calculated overall and by organism. A logistic regression model (Etest R versus I, predictor: zone diameter) was fitted on the 585 paired blood isolates to quantify the probability of an Etest-R result by zone diameter. Analyses used R v4.3 (tidyverse, gt).

This work used retrospectively collected, routinely generated laboratory surveillance data for service evaluation and involved no change to patient care. [Insert local institutional policy reference / ethics committee waiver, as applicable.]

Results
-------

Among the 585 paired blood culture isolates, 494 (84.4%) showed categorical agreement (Table 1). Of 457 disc-I isolates, 450 (98.5%) were confirmed I by Etest and only 7 (1.5%) were reclassified as R. Of 128 disc-R isolates, only 44 (34.4%) were confirmed R; 84 (65.6%) were reclassified as I. This asymmetry held in the two largest organism groups: *E. coli* (62/88, 70.5% of disc-R reclassified; 5/347, 1.4% of disc-I reclassified) and *K. pneumoniae* (21/38, 55.3%; 2/96, 2.1%). Numbers for *K. oxytoca* (n=11), *K. variicola* (n=3) and *P. mirabilis* (n=2) were too small to interpret separately.

Logistic regression showed a steep, highly significant relationship between zone diameter and Etest result (coefficient −0.50, SE 0.056, *P*<0.001; McFadden R² = 0.49; training accuracy 94.7%): each 1 mm increase in zone diameter reduced the odds of an Etest-R result by 39% (OR 0.61, 95% CI 0.54–0.67). At the 17 mm breakpoint itself, fitted P(Etest-R) was 5.1%, falling to 1.2% by 20 mm and to about 0.1% by 25 mm (Figure 1). All seven disc-I/Etest-R discordant isolates had zone diameters within 8 mm of the breakpoint (17–25 mm, median 18 mm), against a susceptible range extending to 34 mm — discordance was concentrated immediately adjacent to the cutoff, not spread unpredictably across it.

Discussion
----------

In blood culture isolates, disc diffusion and Etest disagreed asymmetrically for temocillin. Disc-I results were highly reliable (98.5% confirmed; only 1.5% reclassified to R, clustered within a few millimetres of the breakpoint), supporting continued disc-only reporting for blood isolates called susceptible-with-increased-exposure, without routine Etest confirmation. By contrast, only 34.4% of disc-R isolates were confirmed R by Etest; 65.6% were reclassified as I. Because a disc-R result would ordinarily exclude temocillin as a treatment option,^1,2^ defaulting to disc diffusion alone risks unnecessarily discarding a viable carbapenem-sparing option in most disc-resistant bacteraemic isolates. We suggest reflex Etest (or equivalent MIC) confirmation specifically for disc-R temocillin results in blood cultures, without routine confirmation of disc-I results.

Our R→I rate is considerably higher than major-error rates reported against the older, higher systemic breakpoint,^5^ consistent with the lower EUCAST cutoff itself being a major driver: zone-diameter reading in the few millimetres around a revised breakpoint is known to be susceptible to remeasurement variability,^6^ and our discordant isolates clustered tightly around that cutoff rather than being spread across the full range — consistent with a narrow, measurement-sensitive transition zone rather than a generally unreliable method, and with interlaboratory data showing good overall category agreement for routine temocillin testing.^7^

Limitations: Etest was not ordered on a random sample of blood isolates (21.9% of Etest-tested blood isolates had zone <17 mm versus 8.3% of all blood isolates of these organisms, suggesting reflex testing preferentially targeted smaller, more resistant-looking zones), which concentrates our training data at lower zone diameters without biasing the fitted zone-conditional relationship itself; by restricting to blood cultures we could not assess whether these findings generalise to urine or other specimen types, which had too few paired isolates for separate analysis in this dataset; urinary-tract-source causation was assumed, not confirmed, for these bacteraemic isolates; this is a single-centre, retrospective analysis not yet replicated against other MIC methods (e.g. broth microdilution); and we assessed analytical agreement, not treatment outcomes. Reflex MIC testing for disc-resistant temocillin results in blood cultures should therefore inform, rather than replace, overall clinical judgement.
